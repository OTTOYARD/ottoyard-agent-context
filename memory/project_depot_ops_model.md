Chase's real OTTOYARD-style robotaxi depot ops model (his words, 2026-06-21) — the twin + OTTO-Q must match this, NOT a generic queue:

- **Daytime = relentless 24/7 robotaxi demand.** Vehicles cycle fast: arrive (mostly random, but we have OEM telemetry so arrivals are PREDICTABLE/known in advance), get charged/serviced/cleaned as scheduled, and **redeploy ASAP** — they should be out earning, not parked. Quick turnover. Demand is effectively unlimited during the day.
- **Overnight = demand ~zero** until early-morning commute. Vehicles arrive in **WAVES (~5-10 every few minutes)**, go through full service/charge/clean, then are **HELD in a dedicated parking/staging spot per vehicle** until deployment for the first morning commute.

**Key implication / OTTO-Q behavior fix:** the current decide_tick demand-cap (deploy only to ~55% target, hold 45% as a daytime "buffer") is WRONG for daytime. Correct policy is **day/night-aware**: daytime → redeploy ALL ready vehicles fast (no holding); overnight → pre-stage/hold for the morning commute. The "readiness buffer" is an OVERNIGHT pre-positioning concept, not a daytime one. (This was a misread on my part that Chase corrected.)

**✅ FIXED 2026-06-22 (oq_dn1–dn5):** replaced the static 0.55 target everywhere (decide_tick §2, auto_dispatch, prime_deployment) with `ottoq_deploy_target_fraction(hour, peak)` = `peak × normalized TLC hourly_arrival_rate(hour)` (one calibrated demand curve); normal_day daytime peak → 0.90; added a commute-surge catch-up (≥35%/tick of the deploy gap) so the pre-staged fleet releases fast for AM. Validated over a 13 h flagship run: deployed tracks the curve (30%→4% overnight with 76 pre-staged+charged → surges to 50% by late morning), 0-unsafe throughout. The overnight wave now emerges NATURALLY (no injection). See [[project_energy_e4_charging_fix]] (the energy savings ride on this same overnight wave) + OTTOQ_VISUAL_LAYER_PLAN.md §7 E5.

**Demo proof dimensions Chase wants (ALL of them):** (1) most vehicles safely turned around (throughput + 0 unsafe), (2) fastest turnaround time per vehicle, (3) highest fleet availability (overnight pre-positioned + ready for commute), (4) lowest energy cost (charge into cheap/clean hours, peak-shave, solar/BESS). Load model for the scorekeeper = **relentless 24/7 churn** (most realistic for AV fleets + most dramatic vs manual's ~25-concurrent ceiling).

**Why the first benchmark misled:** a one-shot wave over a long window is too easy — every policy clears it, so OTTO-Q's edge (and the safe demand behavior) didn't show; reckless FIFO even "deployed more" by dumping the lot. The honest test is sustained relentless demand (the "dinner rush"), where manual drowns (~20-30 handled), FIFO makes unsafe calls, and OTTO-Q safely turns around the most (~50-100). See [[project_twin_arrival_cycle]] (B0.5), [[reference_visual_layer_plan]].

**Flagship depot spec (Chase, 2026-06-21):** 1.5-2 acres min; **10 DCFC + 20-30 L2 chargers**; **~100 vehicles** (more is better). HARD CONSTRAINT + differentiator: **one dedicated parking/staging spot per vehicle on-site** (generally around the site perimeter) — so in a safety recall / fleet-wide event EVERY vehicle can return and park safely WITHOUT relying on third-party parking infrastructure. OTTO-Q must never admit/keep more vehicles than the depot can park + service.

**THE distribution/density edge (DEMONSTRATED, not cited — see [[reference_av_depot_research]]):** the specific "50-80 vs 100+" numbers were Chase approximating; the real claim is **efficiency → density**: because OTTO-Q is more energy/charger-efficient (better packing of scarce chargers, less charger-time + energy per vehicle), one site can safely service + charge + clean + park MORE vehicles than naive ops on the same footprint — Chase thinks **150+** is plausible (parking-permitting). Treat as an **efficiency-DERIVED, twin-demonstrated** capability to explore down the road (NOT an industry-cited stat — no public evidence of competitor caps). The honest bridge: utilization edge (45-82× $/kWh swing, evidenced) → more vehicles per fixed charger fleet → higher density. Current twin ~aligned (116 AVs, 10 DCFC, ~35 L2, 100 staging); at recalibration make staging ≥ fleet (1:1 per-vehicle spot, safety-recall) and tune toward demonstrating high density at low energy cost.
